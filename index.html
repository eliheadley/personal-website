About

I'm a computer vision engineer in Atlanta. I graduated from Kennesaw State University in December 2024 with a B.S. in Computer Science and a 4.0, and I've spent my time since then taking deep learning models out of notebooks and into places where they have to keep working — factory floors, edge boxes, and networks that don't always cooperate.

At Georgia-Pacific I work on a manufacturing safety application that grew from its first deployment to more than 70 sites and 500 cameras. My side of that is the production surface: serving models on NVIDIA Triton and DeepStream, containerized Python services on Kubernetes, event-driven integrations over NATS, and the monitoring and alerting that keeps the fleet at 99% uptime. An internal data science team owns training; I own everything that happens after.

Before that I spent a year and a half as an undergraduate research assistant at KSU's College of Computing and Software Engineering, studying how eyelid occlusion from blinking affects iris recognition. That work turned into two published papers and a lasting habit of wanting to measure a thing before claiming it works.

Outside of work I'm usually lifting, cooking, or building something that has no business being built on a Jetson. I'm always happy to talk shop — reach out.

  2. Undergraduate Research Assistant

    Feb 2023 — May 2024

    Kennesaw State University — College of Computing and Software Engineering · Marietta, GA

    • Ran occlusion experiments on frontal and off-angle iris images from ~100 subjects, measuring how eyelid occlusion from blinking affects recognition in both traditional Gabor-based and CNN-based (ResNet) pipelines.
    • Established that training on occluded images largely offsets the accuracy loss, and that partial occlusion can improve performance on severe off-angle images by masking distortion-prone regions.
    • Co-authored both resulting papers on a three-person research team.
